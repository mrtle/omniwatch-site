Fair questions

Why don't you just scan for me?

A tool that takes a child's name and returns a profile works just as well for a stranger as for a parent. So OmniWatch never collects or compiles anything. It builds the searches, and you run them and decide what matters.

Where does my child's information go?

Nowhere. This page has no accounts, no analytics and no server that receives what you type. Your audit is saved in this browser's storage so you can come back to it, and "Erase my audit" wipes it.

What's in the Takedown Kit?

For each thing you found: the request to send, already written with your details, the place to send it, the rule it relies on where one applies, and a follow-up date. Plus the standard set every family can use: people-search opt-outs, a school directory opt-out letter, Google's removal routes, and a kind message for relatives.

Will everything get removed?

No one can promise that. Sites covered by privacy law have to respond, most schools, teams and relatives say yes when asked clearly, and Google removes images of minors on request with some exceptions. News coverage and public records are the hardest.

Is this legal advice?

No. OmniWatch is an information tool. The letters cite rules that commonly apply, but your situation may differ. For anything serious, such as harassment, threats or a custody dispute, talk to a lawyer or the police.

Refunds?

If the kit doesn't help, reply to your receipt within 14 days for a full refund.
